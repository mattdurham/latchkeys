What every Latchkey knows

Everything you need to play — who you are, how the dice work, what your moves do, and how a night in Deep Lake actually runs. No spoilers, no Keeper advice. If it’s on this page, your character can know it.

1OverviewWho you are

You’re a Deep Lake Latchkey — now in your early twenties, back in town for the summer. You grew up here in the ’80s and early ’90s with a spare key around your neck and too much unsupervised time. You met the other Latchkeys online, bonding over a shared obsession: TV Odyssey. This summer you’re renting a house together on Rodenbecker Street to figure out what actually happened to it.

Start of game: Pick a name and a rough backstory. Your pre-made sheet already has your style, abilities, and personal moves filled in. You’ll define a few more details together in Session One.

You each carry some childhood hurt. It can be big or small — what matters is how it shaped you now.

2The settingDeep Lake, Kentucky

  • Remote town, fewer than 5,000 year-round.
  • The lake doubles the population in summer — vacationers and seasonal workers.
  • Every current mystery somehow circles back to TV Odyssey.
  • You operate out of the rented house on Rodenbecker Street.
The house matters. Your Corner of the House holds real items you brought with you. An unmarked item gives you advantage once when you use it — after that it’s still there for fiction, but the bonus is spent.

3The mystery at the centerTV Odyssey — Channel 94

TV Odyssey was Deep Lake’s public access studio. Anyone could book time and put a show on cable channel 94 — and with almost no oversight, people did. Low-budget, strange, and local:

Happy Jack the Lumberjack & Friends • Voyages to the Astral with Lunaria Gomez • Poetry SLAM • Gastronomica • Daily Affirmations with David Tilbert — and more that don’t fit anywhere else.

What happened: In 1992 the building was simply gone one morning. The official story is it burned down, even though there’s no evidence for it. No one in town will talk about it. Press them and you get the same line: “It burned in the early ’90s.”
What’s left: Odyssey tapes — purple clamshell VHSes with day-glo pyramids and torii gates on the insert. Extremely rare, valuable to collectors. Finding one on a 12+ Meddling result is a big deal.
Content note — tap to show

The game starts with nostalgic investigation and gets darker. It can include cosmic horror, ritual language, childhood trauma, sexism and sexual desire, and consumer-culture satire. Violence is not described graphically. Talk through boundaries and lines/veils with your group before play.

4At the tableHow it works

Goal

Solve the mysteries in front of you. No one — including the Keeper — knows the “right” answer ahead of time. You use the Clues you’ve found to build a theory, and the Answer a Question move decides if you’re correct and what happens next.

The conversation

Most of the game is just talking. The Keeper describes the world and asks what you do. You say what your Latchkey does. Keep going until what you describe triggers a move — then stop, read the move, roll if it says to, and narrate the outcome. Then go back to the conversation.

The big rule: Players roll, the Keeper doesn’t. Say what you do, not what happens. Ask questions any time. And you can always choose to back down before a risky roll when the move offers it.

Roleplay is worldbuilding

When the Keeper asks you to fill in a detail — what you notice, remember, or brought with you — just say it. Specifics are fuel.

5Your numbersAbilities

When a move says to roll, add one of these (from −3 to +3 on your sheet). The Keeper has final say which one fits.

Vitality VIT

Strength, speed, endurance, athleticism. Sprinting, grappling, breaking in, carrying someone out.

Composure COM

Calm under pressure, steady hands, focus. Resisting fear, lying still, eavesdropping.

Reason RSN

Study, analysis, research. Reading a scene, library work, connecting dots.

Presence PRE

Charm, intimidation, performance. Working a room, sweet-talking, drawing attention.

Sensitivity SEN

Occult and supernatural. The Keeper may call for this when something uncanny shapes the action.

6DiceHow to roll

  1. Roll 2d6.
  2. Add the relevant ability modifier.
  3. Compare to the outcome bands below.
ResultWhat it means
6− MissThings get worse. The Keeper reacts or escalates.
7–9 Hit*You do it, but with a complication, cost, or hard choice.
10–11 HitClean success. You do what you intended.
12+ CritStrong success — clean + an extra benefit, advantage, or useful info.

*A hit is 7 or higher. A miss is 6 or lower. Many moves key off those words.

Advantage & disadvantage

Advantage: Roll 3d6, keep the highest two, then add ability. Often from a move, or from using an unmarked Corner item.
Disadvantage: Roll 3d6, keep the lowest two, then add ability. Often from a Condition, or Keeper-called circumstances.
They cancel. If you’d have both, roll normal 2d6. They never stack — two Conditions + one advantage is still just normal 2d6.

7Your safety netThe Keys

Your sheet has two Keys: Key of the Child (who you were) and Key of Desolation (what TV Odyssey is doing to you). The Desolation track is serious — the last box retires your Latchkey.

Turning a Key: After the dice and after the Keeper narrates the outcome, you may turn a Key to raise the result one tier — miss → 7–9 → 10–11 → 12+. Mark the next box on that Key and follow its prompt before the end of the session. We then re-narrate: what was just described still happened as a felt “other timeline,” but now we play the new result instead.
Example — why the Keeper still narrates the bad outcome first

Ryan is caught by a corrupt sheriff after exposing him. The player rolls a miss. The Keeper describes the sheriff throwing Ryan off a cliff to silence him — you feel that timeline together. The player then turns a Key: the miss becomes 7–9. Now Ryan escapes, but drops evidence the sheriff can use to track him. Turning a Key is like putting your thumb on the page of a Choose-Your-Own-Adventure to see the bad ending, then going back and choosing differently — on purpose.

Special case: the Answer a Question move can only be boosted by Keys if every Latchkey turns one.

8When you actThe five moves

Name what you do. If it matches one of these, do what the move says. Day vs Night is about danger, not the clock.

›Night Move Dangerous scenesRisk + fear

When you do something risky or face something you fear in a dangerous scene — regardless of actual time — use this.

  1. Name what you fear will happen if you fail or lose your nerve.
  2. The Keeper tells you how it’s worse than that.
  3. Choose: back down, or go through with it and roll + ability.
  • 10+: You do it / hold steady. Say what it looks like.
  • 7–9: You do it / hold steady, but there’s a complication or cost. Keeper says what.
  • 12+: As 10+, plus an extra benefit or advantage. Keeper says what.

Consequences here are more perilous than Day. Think: breaking into the killer’s house, even at noon.

›Day Move Lower-stakesRisk, lighter

When you do something risky or face something you fear in a lower-stakes scene — regardless of actual time — roll + ability.

  • 10+: You do it / hold steady. Say what it looks like.
  • 7–9: You do it / hold steady, but there’s a complication or cost. Keeper says what.
  • 12+: As 10+, plus an extra benefit or advantage.

Quieter danger. A tense conversation after dark can still be a Day Move.

›Meddling Move Find thingsClues

When you search, research, or gather information — say how, then roll + the ability that fits the method.

  • Hit (7+): You find a Clue. Keeper tells you what it is.
  • 7–9: Also a complication — with the Clue or the search.
  • 12+: Also an Odyssey tape or unusual local history. Keeper chooses.

Match fiction to ability: Presence to sweet-talk a deputy; Composure to eavesdrop; Reason to dig in the library. Keeper decides ties.

Getting Clues from people: Residents don’t like talking about Deep Lake’s mysteries. Getting a Clue from someone will likely require getting on their good side — or coercing them — first. You’ll need to earn their trust, do them a favor, or apply pressure before they open up.
Nighttime is fruitful — and dangerous: Investigating nighttime activities can yield Clues more easily and in greater numbers, but with significantly more danger. What you find after dark often comes with a cost.
›Nostalgic Move Heart-to-heartHeal

When you share an intimate moment while one of you is waxing nostalgic about something that Takes You Back (a game, song, show, etc.):

  • Each of you may clear an appropriate Condition.
  • The Latchkey who was not waxing nostalgic also finds a relevant Clue — you tell the Keeper what it is. It can’t solve the mystery by itself.

Risk-free Clue, and the best way to shake Conditions.

›Answer a Question Solve itConsensus

When you have at least half the Clues a Question’s Complexity requires, talk it out and reach a consensus. Then roll:

2d6 + Clues used − Question Complexity

  • 10+: You’re correct. Its Opportunity can be pursued.
  • 7–9: You’re correct, but the Keeper adds a complication and/or makes the Opportunity more dangerous.
  • 6−: You’re wrong. The Keeper reacts.
  • 12+: The Big Man appears while you pursue the Opportunity.
No advantage/disadvantage on this roll. No canonical answers. Your Clues + your theory are the answer. Boosting tiers here requires everyone to turn a Key.

9Your sheetCharacter — quick anatomy

Identity & style

  • Name: Circle or make your own.
  • Style: Outlined on your sheet — your outlook, not a costume.
  • Takes You Back: 3 nostalgic touchstones (songs, games, shows). Triggers the Nostalgic move.

Conditions

Traits that give disadvantage when they’d plausibly get in the way.

  • Max 3. A fourth forces you to turn a Key instead.
  • Cleared by moves or Keeper instruction.

Latchkey moves

You can only use a Latchkey move that’s actually printed on your sheet — it’s how sheets differ.

Your Corner of the House

Real items in your corner of the Rodenbecker house. Function as gear and color.

  • An unmarked item → advantage once when relevant.
  • After use, mark it. It stays for fiction, bonus is spent.
  • Generally can’t be unmarked. Add items in Session One or when a move/Keeper says so.

Keys — what marking a box means

When you turn a Key, mark its next box and follow that box’s prompt before the end of the current session. Key of the Child explores flashbacks; Key of Desolation tracks what the station is doing to you — its final box retires the Latchkey.

10How a session flowsThe play cycle

DAWN → DAY → DUSK → NIGHT → (repeat)

Day / Night are about rhythm and stakes, not the clock. A new Day can be days or weeks later in fiction.

☀ Dawn — upkeep

1. Collect Rewards if a Threat was resolved. 2. Resolve Dawn moves / Key prompts. 3. Optionally resolve any lingering Key prompts (must by session end).
◐ Day — investigate & live

If fewer than three mysteries are active, the Keeper presents a new one. Follow your interests: relaxed investigation, character scenes, nostalgia, Clues. Mysteries cannot be resolved during Day. Continue until the Keeper moves to Dusk.
◑ Dusk — pivot to night

1. Resolve Dusk moves. 2. Decide together whether to watch an Odyssey tape (tie → Keeper decides). 3. Each player says what their Latchkey will do for Night, then gets one revision. 4. If watching, Keeper reads the tape open and assigns prompts.
● Night — danger

Without a tape: Continues like Day but faster and more dangerous until the Keeper advances.

With a tape: Loop: resolve a prompt → brief Latchkey reaction scene → next prompt, until prompts run out. Stay near the viewing spot; scenes are bathroom-break short; dice rarely. Budget ~20–30 min real time.

✓One glanceQuick reminders

  • Say what you do; don’t narrate the result before the roll.
  • Ask the Keeper questions — you can’t investigate what you don’t ask about.
  • Hit = 7+, miss = 6−. Most moves key off those.
  • You can turn a Key after the narration to bump the result one tier.
  • Day vs Night is about danger, not time of day.
  • An unmarked Corner item = advantage once.
  • Max 3 Conditions — the 4th costs a Key.
  • Keep Clues visible. You need at least half Complexity to attempt Answer a Question.

★Pick one — no duplicatesLatchkey Moves — choose one each

At the start of the game each player chooses one Latchkey Move. No two Latchkeys can ever have the same move. It’s your personal edge — the thing that makes your Latchkey different at the table. Read through and pick the one that fits your character.

How to choose: Skim the titles, then read the full text for a few that call to you. If your table has a sheet with moves already picked (like the pre-gens), you can keep that one or swap to any below — just no duplicates.
Where’s the Beef?

You are a little bigger and a little stronger than most. Increase your Vitality by 1 (max 3). You always use Vitality on rolls associated with actions connected to the Big Man.

Sight Beyond Sight

Whether you call it premonitions, omens, or just a hunch, you glimpse the future. The first time you enter a Location, ask the Keeper “What is about to happen?” They answer truthfully, though not necessarily completely. If you have marked The Sandstone Arch, you can also ask “Why is it about to happen?”

This Is Your Brain

You are a little smarter than most, and your logic holds in dark places. Increase your Reason by 1 (max 3). When you mark The Fathomless Well, do not decrease your Reason.

Robot in Disguise

You came to Deep Lake with gadgets. Add to Your Corner of the House: Laptop, Walkie Talkies, GPS, Camcorder, Voice-Activated Audio Recorder.

Come With Me If You Want to Live

You always spot the exits. Once per session, when you and any number of others face grave harm, you can avoid it by narrating how you lead everyone to safety.

They Think He’s a Righteous Dude

Motorheads, dweebs, and wastoids trust you. What is it? Once per session, when you do the Meddling Move in a place occupied by losers and social outcasts, you find an additional Clue, even on a miss.

Knowing is Half the Battle

You’re a deep repository of knowledge, some of it useful. Once per session, you can introduce a real-world fact about something in the scene. That fact is a Clue.

A Winner is You

You were a loser back then, but not anymore! People respect you now. Why? Increase your Presence by 1 (max 3).

Say Hello to My Little Friend

You have an exceptionally smart dog with a strong bond. What breed? Give it a name and add it to Your Corner of the House. When you use the dog to get advantage on a roll, leave it unmarked.

I Am Error

You can mark The Chromatic Desert whenever you want. The other Latchkeys don’t forget you, and you can still interact if a TV is nearby — you appear on whatever is being watched and can hear people speak to you through the TV. When the Latchkeys watch an Odyssey tape, your scenes can take place inside the tape.

Who Ya Gonna Call?

You’re active on the Ghost Bro Mystery Show forums. When you post a question about a Deep Lake mystery, roll with Presence. On a 10+, a commenter has a good answer or Clue and shares it freely. On a 7–9, as above, but they want you to do something first.

Thank You for Being a Friend

You’re easy to open up to. When you do the Nostalgic Move, the other Latchkey can clear a second Condition or they can take the Condition: Feeling Good. They can clear Feeling Good at any time to get advantage on a die roll.

And They’re Always Glad You Came

You have family still in Deep Lake. Who? Any rolls they help with are at advantage. If you’re in immediate danger and can make it to their house, you’re safe until at least the next morning.

You Never Know What You’re Going to Get

You have a research background. What is it? Once per session, when you do the Meddling Move by looking through books, microfiche, old records, or online archives, you find an additional Clue, even on a miss.

Have You Ever Danced with the Devil

You brought tools for black magic. Add to Your Corner of the House: Candles, Grimoire, Small Brazier, Athame, Poppet.

Strange Things are Afoot at the Circle K

You believe in the power of convenience stores. If something typically found at a convenience store would help and you take time to go get it, say what it is and add it to Your Corner of the House.

Just Say No

Something about your past makes you good at handling cops. What is it? Whenever you’re harassed by police or need to talk your way out of law-enforcement trouble, all associated rolls are at advantage.

What Nintendon’t

Once per session, you can take a 12+ on a single roll related to an action so long as you do it with cockiness or style.

Next Sunday A.D.

Once per Day phase, you and at least one other player can do an out-of-character narration of an upcoming scene like movie commentary. At least one thing you say will be true about the scene — tell the Keeper what it is. They may make other parts true too.

19 moves total. If your table has more than 6 players, the Keeper can allow duplicates or homebrew — otherwise, first pick sticks.
